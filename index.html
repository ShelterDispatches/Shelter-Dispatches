Hard Repairs at Scale: Why Category 2 Can't Wait Any Longer

Three years into the response, the easy work is done. Light and medium repairs have been delivered to tens of thousands of households across the Crescent Zone — and the need for them is visibly declining. What remains is harder, more expensive, and structurally more complex: buildings where a window replacement won't cut it, where the roof structure has collapsed or the wall has lost its load-bearing capacity. Category 2 demand is accelerating, driven not only by new damage but by the growing backlog of households that were assessed, found ineligible for Cat. 1, and have been waiting ever since.

About this journal

Shelter work
from the field,
not the office.

Shelter Dispatches is a practitioner's journal on humanitarian shelter response in Ukraine. It covers field methodology, programme design, donor trends, policy developments, and the unglamorous realities of delivering repairs and housing solutions at scale in an active conflict zone.

It is written from the field — from site assessments in Kharkiv, coordination meetings in Dnipro, and technical reviews in Zaporizhzhia — with the goal of being useful to the people doing the work, not just reporting on it.

Content draws on experience implementing Shelter Cluster SOP-aligned programmes across 8 oblasts of Ukraine, covering Category 1 and Category 2 household repairs, CCCM collective centre rehabilitation, social housing, and early recovery.
